Inventory reconciliation

All 145 discovery entries are on the Nov 3 ballot. There are no additions in any state. The one removal (Arkansas "Ballot Measure Rights", rejected July 30) was never in the inventory. Each measure was verified against the Secretary of State or the official voter guide. Corrections and notable facts:

  • California:
    • The billionaire tax is Prop 40 (25-0024A1), and voter ID is Prop 39 (25-0007A1).
    • Prop 43 is the Legislature's ACA 22. The Howard Jarvis "Local Taxpayer Protection Act" initiative (#1983) was withdrawn June 25, and ACA 13 was removed.
    • Props 41 and 42 can void Prop 40 if they get more yes votes.
  • Washington: the income-tax measure is IP26-645 (initiative to the people), not IL26-645.
  • North Dakota: the court struck the term-limits measure, so the 60% measure is now Measure 1 and free school meals is Measure 2.
  • New Mexico: the inventory's bond amounts are wrong; official figures are $21.6M, $19.3M and $352.2M.
  • Missouri:
    • Amendment 3 (2026) is new and would repeal 2024's Amendment 3. Markets on the 2022 and 2024 votes are rejected.
    • Prop A: YES approves the 2025 map (HB 1); NO keeps the 2022 boundaries.
  • Nevada: Questions 6 and 7 are the required second votes. Both first passed in 2024; only the 2026 vote counts here.
  • Approval rules recorded verbatim:
    • Florida: 60%.
    • Colorado: 55%, except Amendment 87, which only repeals language.
    • Tennessee: YES must also exceed half of all votes cast for governor.
    • Wyoming: a majority of all ballots cast.
    • Minnesota: a blank counts as NO.
    • Nebraska: 35% of total votes.
    • Massachusetts: 30% turnout rule.
    • New Hampshire: two-thirds.
    • Washington: votes on the measure must be at least ⅓ of all votes cast.
  • Other states:
    • Idaho's gun question is advisory and multiple-choice.
    • Utah: A is the publication amendment and B is the 60% tax amendment.
    • Maryland Question 3's wording was replaced by court order on Sept 3.
    • Wisconsin's three amendments are on the November ballot, not April's.
    • Arizona 143, 145 and 212 were struck by courts.
  • Access gaps: the official sites for Arizona, Nevada, Alaska, Florida, Georgia, North Carolina, Rhode Island, Michigan and Ohio blocked this network, and Arizona and Alaska blocked a second fetch path too. Those identities rest on Ballotpedia quoting official text, legislature or county copies, or news. That is why the Arizona and Alaska creation entries are held.

Creation plan

Market shape and rules

One binary per measure: "Will {State} {Designation} ({short subject}) be approved in the November 3, 2026 election?" Every description uses the same template:

  • the official approval rule, quoted verbatim;
  • only the 2026 vote counts, with second votes stated;
  • advisory questions mean voter approval only. Only a measure whose verified type is advisory is described that way. The validator rejects a binding measure described as advisory, after Idaho HJR 4 was wrongly marked advisory (its notes mention the separate state-gun question);
  • the certified result controls, recounts included;
  • N/A if the measure is removed, its votes are not counted, or the vote is postponed;
  • later court rulings do not change the result.

Unnumbered measures, such as North Carolina's amendments, are named by subject. Markets close 2026-11-04 12:00 UTC, after every state has closed its polls. This matches the race manifest.

Seeds and liquidity

  • 32 seeds come from cited 2026 polls or prior results. 70 are 50% with no basis, and all are flagged for review. 20 cited seeds are also flagged, as judgement calls on the evidence. Seeds are not forecasts.
  • Default tier is 1,000. 10,000 is recommended for 13 measures with evidence: CA Props 3, 41, 42 and 45; CO Amendment 87; Iowa Amendment 1; Idaho Prop 1; Maryland Question 3; Missouri Amendment 3; Montana I-194; Nebraska Initiative 440; Virginia Question 1; Washington IP26-645.

Duplicate recheck

The read-only online recheck last ran against the public API on 2026-10-03, finishing about 10:40 UTC, for both manifests. It used no credentials and made no writes.

  • Result: no reserved ID is taken. None of the 102 ballot markets or 225 race markets has an equivalent or ambiguous match.
  • Answers now included: searches send includeLiteAnswers=true; before that, every multi-answer market came back with no answers. The first run with answers flagged 22 race entries (33 hits) and 2 measures. All were unrelated answer lists: NASCAR drivers, the next Speaker, another state's Senate race, the Mayor of London, the Milei mega-market. The classifier and reviewed rejections now handle them; see the review section below. A live query confirms answers come back, for example 19 for the Midwest House portfolio.

This recheck only catches markets created after the snapshot. Apply runs it again just before each write.

Script changes

  • election-market-creation.ts adds a ballot-measure entry kind with:
    • measure identity: state, official designation, aliases, approval rule, certifier and official source;
    • a YES-means-approved proposition and a BALLOT_MEASURES dashboard row.
    Its own manifest kind and series keep it apart from the race manifest; the validator refuses to mix the two. The race manifest's dry run is unchanged: 225 markets, Ṁ306,000.
  • Validation:
    • the exact question shape, the state and the designation;
    • the threshold quoted verbatim;
    • N/A, recount, certification and court rules;
    • second-vote wording, and advisory wording that never promises a law;
    • 50% seeds flagged for review.
  • Duplicate check:
    • matches the designation and aliases, and checks portfolio answers individually by answer ID;
    • treats a different state, year, or a qualification-only question as unrelated;
    • holds opposite wording, repeal wording, and conditional, combined, margin, court or implementation questions for review.
  • Kept from the race manifest: dry run by default, paginated search, apply gates, finite caps, deterministic idempotency, resume, and a stop on ambiguous outcomes.
  • Fix to the shared duplicate search (affects the race manifest too).
    • The PR paged /v0/search-markets by offset up to 10,000, but the API rejects any offset above 1,000 with HTTP 400. A term with 1,100 or more results, such as "Question 1", made the online check crash.
    • Race terms name a state and an office, so they rarely reach that depth, but both manifests use the same code path.
    • It now pages with sort=newest and a beforeTime cursor, as the API's own error message recommends. Pages are 100 rows, because the server answers a failed query with HTTP 200 and no rows, and small pages stay inside its statement timeout. Rows created in the same millisecond at a page boundary are re-read and de-duplicated by ID.
    • It still fails closed on a page that adds nothing new, rows without a creation time, a 404 or malformed result, or more than 20,000 results.
    • Every ballot search term now names the state. A bare alias such as "Prop 1" is searched as "California Prop 1".
  • Tests:
    • 18 new mocked ballot tests and 8 new tests for search, classification and apply safety; all 48 tests in the suite pass. The CLI type-checks.
    • 6 tests for the fourth-tab model.
    • The web project type-checks.
