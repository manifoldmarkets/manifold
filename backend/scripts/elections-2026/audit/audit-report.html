Scope, method and gaps

  • Inventory. 2026 House, Senate and governor tables (Wikipedia wikitext, which cites each state’s certified list), then checked against official sources: CA SOS certified list (8/27/2026), FL DOE, MA list and PD43, LA SOS calendar and Act 7, Georgia code, Vermont constitution, Missouri SOS and the Supreme Court order in 26A388, the House Clerk vacancy list. 435 seats, 35 Senate races, 36 governors confirmed.
  • Market discovery. Every non-deleted market open after June 2026 whose title, full-text description or any answer row mentions an office, district, party or election (5,672 markets, 22,597 answers), plus a full-name search for every listed candidate across all 33,950 open binary and multiple-choice markets (which found the CA-11 Wiener binary, CA-17, CA-24, CO-3, CO-4, NC-9, NY-13 and Alaska governor markets the keyword pass missed). All 1,561 public comments on the 815 shortlisted markets and all 1,544 live limit orders, with maker balances, were read.
  • Depth. Pool liquidity, per-answer liquidity and live limit-order depth are reported separately. Ṁ10 and Ṁ100 buys on both sides were simulated with the repository’s own bet code (common/new-bet: CPMM, sum-to-one arbitrage, limit-order matching with maker balance checks) for 96 sources.
  • Gaps. elections.alaska.gov, sos.ga.gov, senate.gov, SC VREMS, elections.wi.gov, the live MA candidate page and the Idaho candidate search were blocked from this location; Alaska and Idaho rest on news reports (Alaska Public Media, ADN). The CA June primary Statement of Vote was not fetched; CA-29’s seed uses AP figures. The atlas’s Alabama shapes were not checked against the 2023 plan. Comments were read only for shortlisted markets.

Creation plan (dry run only)

225 races have no suitable market after the full search. These are 224 House seats (mostly safe seats outside the portfolios: Illinois 16, Georgia 13, Pennsylvania 12, North Carolina 11) and the Rhode Island governor race. A further four uncontested seats carry draft payloads but are excluded from apply. Every entry records its race key, search evidence, rejected alternatives, the exact payload, answer metadata, seed basis, close time, cost, topics and the dashboard mapping row.

Market shape

  • Ballot-party multi (223): one answer per major party actually on the ballot, plus “Another party or independent”. The party is the winner’s ballot line, with fusion counted under the nominating party and later switches ignored. Settles on the certified final round (Georgia runoff, Louisiana Dec 12 round, Maine and Alaska ranked choice), or earlier on an AP call with nothing pending. Nominee replacement keeps the party answer; postponement waits and cancellation N/As.
  • Candidate multi (2): CA-29 (two Democrats; no Other answer, since write-ins are barred) and Rhode Island governor (Foulkes, Guckian, Block, Another candidate). Party is metadata, never the label.

Seeds and close

  • Seeds are not forecasts. They come from the district’s 2026 Cook PVI plus a 3-point incumbent-party bonus through a logistic with a 4-point scale, assuming a neutral national environment. They are clamped to 3–97%, with 2% for other candidates.
  • One-major-party ballots seed 95/5. CA-29 uses primary shares; Rhode Island is reviewer-set from the post-primary poll.
  • Markets close 2026-11-03 23:59 UTC. Runoff races need the creator to extend the close.

Duplicate recheck

The dry run was repeated against the public API on 2026-10-03, using only unauthenticated GETs. For each entry it checked the reserved ID and ran 6–8 searches by district code, ordinal, state and candidate name. No market exists at any reserved ID, and no equivalent market was found. 38 entries were held as ambiguous, covering 17 distinct markets:

  • five unresolved 2024 Oregon House markets whose titles carry no year;
  • the Louisiana and Virginia seat-count markets;
  • a conditional IL-9 market;
  • name collisions: James Bond, three non-election Ilhan Omar questions, an ACX event, a Civilization game, and a vote-share comparison.

All 17 are now recorded in the manifest as reviewed rejections with reasons, so they will not block an apply run. Any new match still will.

The script

  • Dry run is the default. It needs no credentials and the client cannot write. It produces a readable report, payload and cost JSON, a plan, and a dashboard mapping with PENDING:<raceKey> IDs.
  • Apply has five gates. It requires a reviewed manifest (reviewer and date), an explicit --apply, --creator-username matching the key’s /v0/me, --max-mana for the run, and an approved cap across runs. It stops before any create that would exceed a cap or the balance.
  • Before each create. It reads the reserved ID read-only, then searches by race identity (office, state, district, year, round, candidate names). An equivalent market is skipped and recorded; an ambiguous one is held for review.
  • No duplicates. Each entry gets a deterministic idempotencyKey; the create API uses it as the contract ID and rejects a second create. State is written after every request.
  • Failures. Rate limits wait and retry with the same key. An ambiguous timeout is reconciled with a read-only GET and then stops the run. A 4xx isolates the entry, and a 401 or 403 stops the run.
  • Tests. 20 mocked tests cover dry run (no writes), duplicate avoidance, both budget caps, partial failure and resume, ambiguous timeouts (landed and unconfirmed) and rate limits. All pass.
