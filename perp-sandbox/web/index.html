Can funding pay for an asset that drifts up?

The question behind SPY and QQQ. Equities carry a real expected return; if longs collect it and funding cannot claw it back, the long side is +EV by construction and someone has to fund that.

The headline number

Break-even by leverage

A long earns drift on notional and pays funding on margin. Per unit of margin it collects drift × leverage and pays funding × 1, so the funding needed to stay EV-neutral scales linearly with leverage while the cap does not move at all.

Max fair leverage against drift

Above this line the long side is +EV no matter how the imbalance sits, because the required rate exceeds f_max. Below it, funding can in principle compensate — at the imbalance ratio shown.

Where a one-sided book settles

A short's funding income is amplified by the pool ratio — the transfer is f × the long pool, spread over a smaller short pool — which is what makes crowding self-correcting. This is modelling layered on top of prod's math, not prod behaviour: only the funding rate itself comes from common/.

Short leverage cancels out of the break-even, so it changes each side's numbers but never the settling ratio.

What a subsidy buys

Added mana raises each side's cover, and cover is what caps open interest. That is the part worth thinking about carefully before giga-subsidising anything.

How much of BTC is house money?

Escrow is trader margin plus everything Manifold put in. Because a newly opened position reserves exactly its own margin against the opposing side, trader margin cannot finance capacity at all — every unit of cover comes from subsidy, taker fees, funding, or the opposing side losing money.

What a leverage cap would do

Holding every trader's margin fixed, so this is the conservative reading — if they instead posted more margin to keep their exposure, the house does better still.

The house edge that already exists

The taker fee is 10 bps of notional at open, paid into the trader's own side pool. It is the only systematic inflow the market has.

Structuring SPY, QQQ and GOLD

Two independent constraints bind the leverage cap and the answer is the smaller. Drift: a long earns it on notional and pays funding on margin, so break-even leverage is f_max ÷ drift. Volatility: liquidation sits 1/leverage away, so ordinary noise must not reach it. They bind different assets — BTC is volatility-limited and driftless; SPY is the reverse.

Where each book settles, and what it costs

Capping leverage at the drift break-even is not enough — the settling ratio only charges the long half the cap, so it still clears f_max/2 a year. The last column is the cap that actually neutralises it.

Twelve-month house P&L, as a distribution

Escrow moves by exactly three things: + taker fees, − net OI × return (the house is, in aggregate, short whatever traders are net long), and − bad debt on feed discontinuities. Funding contributes zero — it is a transfer inside the same escrow, so it matters only through its effect on net OI.

Returns are block-bootstrapped from the 368 real daily returns of the live oracle feed, then de-meaned: the sample year ran at −47.5%/yr, and leaving that in would manufacture an edge that is really just "BTC happened to fall in 2025-26". Set the drift you actually believe in below.

Does adding liquidity improve the odds?

If open interest, volume and net exposure all scale with house money, then expected profit and risk scale by the same factor — so the ratio between them, and therefore the odds, do not move at all.
