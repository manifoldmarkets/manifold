# Yap shared discussions and re-Yaps

Combined implementation, updated 18 November 2026. No production deployment.

## Behaviour

- Re-Yap shares a stable original item. One active share per person; undo does not delete content. Re-sharing can bump it at most once per 24 hours, including undo/re-add cycles.
- Quote creates a distinct authored post, on Yap only: a quote never comments on the market, and replies to it stay on Yap. Unrelated takes that link the same market stay separate. The Yap button in a market's comment box is the one place that posts to both: "Also comment on the market" is ticked there by default, and unticking it posts to Yap only. When the original can no longer be shown on Yap (removed, taken off Yap, hidden, or behind a block), the quote shows "Original post unavailable." with no author, no text and no link. A quoted comment or trade is treated as a quoted Yap is. This covers the quote box only: a removed post's own place in its thread still names its author, as it did before this work.
- A market comment and its Yap post are one message with one set of likes, and replies made on either side appear on both. The market comment is the record: it is written, edited and stored under the market's rules. Yap shows a copy of it, cut to what Yap's format holds (paragraphs, mentions, visible links, up to four images, 2,000 characters). A comment that starts its own thread on Yap shows its market under it, as a post with a market attached does, and a quote of it shows the market too. The @name that the market's Reply button puts at the start of a reply reads as that person's name on Yap.
- Commenting on a market never depends on Yap. A comment is saved as written first; if its thread, trade or market has a Yap discussion that still takes messages, it then joins that discussion as a copy. Likes made on the market are the market's own reactions, which Yap reads.
- Editing: the author or an admin, with no time limit, on the market. On Yap the same people can edit a post that has a market comment, unless the comment has formatting Yap cannot hold; that one is edited on its market. Standalone Yaps keep the 30-minute window.
- Removing: an author who removes their post takes it off Yap only. Their market comment stays, because authors cannot delete market comments, and it is not copied to Yap again: sharing it from the market is refused with "This comment's author took it off Yap". A moderator's removal deletes both, under the market's rules: not a comment by an admin or moderator, and not while banned from moderating comments.
- Hiding: a market creator's hide collapses the comment on their market and does not reach Yap. An admin's or moderator's hide also hides the post on Yap, and their unhide restores it.
- Mod comments stay on their market: they cannot be re-Yapped, quoted or copied to Yap.
- Replies use normal commenting eligibility; publishing and re-Yapping require membership. Public discussions can be read while signed out. Reading never writes. A market's or a trade's card lists the market's comments, and a comment lists its replies, whether or not each has a post on Yap: a comment that has none is read from its market and shown as the post it would be, under the id that post will have. It gets its post when someone first acts on it on Yap (a reply, a like, a re-Yap or a quote, a follow, an edit, a removal or a report), or replies to it on its market. Taking back a like that was made on the market needs no post. Comments that can never be on Yap are not listed, counted or given a post: mod comments, hidden or deleted comments, those on a market that is no longer public, and those whose author has a block with the author of the post they would sit under, of its thread or of the market. The "on Yap" link beside a comment on its market appears once the comment has its post.
- A comment shared before its market or trade keeps its original discussion and subscribers. The market and trade cards can list that comment without moving its thread. A trade reply joins the market discussion when the trade has no card of its own.
- Posting or replying on Yap, or choosing Follow, makes you follow that thread. Mute and unmute apply to that thread's Yap alert and to nothing else: the market's own alerts follow the market's settings, whether the Yap thread is muted or not. Commenting on a market does not make you follow: when a market comment gets its post on Yap, because someone else acted on it or shared it or because its author just wrote it, the author's notifications stay as the market sends them. Re-Yap notifications aggregate for the original author.
- Explore includes native Yap cards. Shared market/comment/trade items have stable identities even when shared without commentary.
- Ranking counts independent people across comments, Yaps and re-Yaps, excluding market creators and bots. Mirroring and repeated shares cannot multiply the signal.

## Local testing

Open http://localhost:3000/yap. The local API is http://localhost:8088 and uses DEV Firebase and the DEV database.

1. Re-Yap the same post from two accounts: one feed item, original author retained, two shares and one discussion. Undo one share; the original and replies remain. Undo/re-add within 24 hours does not advance its bump time.
2. Quote the post with your own text: a separate authored post and conversation, linked to the original.
3. Share a market comment, reply from Yap, then reply from the market. Check placement and shared likes. Edit the comment on the market with bold text and a list: the market keeps the formatting, Yap shows the plain copy, and Yap's Edit sends you back to the market. Remove your own post on Yap: the comment stays on the market. Hide a shared comment as the market's creator: it collapses on the market and stays on Yap. Hide it as a moderator: it goes from both. Standalone Yaps retain the 30-minute edit window.
4. Follow/mute/unmute the thread from the Yap menu. Participants get one grouped in-app alert for replies instead of the market's in-app alert; email and push still follow their market preferences. Muting the thread stops the grouped Yap alert and nothing else: someone who also follows the market then gets the market's own alerts again, on the channels their market settings allow. Other market followers retain their existing notifications.
5. Look at Explore for native Yap cards. Open a Yap URL while signed out. Confirm moderator-comment mode and Post to Yap remain mutually exclusive.

The count shows active unique sharers. Re-Yapping does not create a new authored post or silently subscribe the sharer to replies. Quotes remain separate, even when they refer to the same market.

The label is never used: a username can change hands. A deleted account or one behind a block stays unnamed, and posts written on Yap are still held to user ids. The lookup finds each comment by its own id.   - The migration's recount writes only the comments whose counts are wrong, so running it again rewrites nothing that is right.
  - The users a read needs are found through the primary key. Written as it was, and as it is on main, that lookup read the whole users table on every read: 357 ms with 300,000 users on a local database, and 0.1 ms now.
  - The reply count's plan test requires a lookup by parent or by comment.

- Fifth review round, 20 November (not yet on DEV), after a timing run made for the follow-up that takes copying out of reads. A read's scan for comments not yet on Yap asked for a card's top-level comments with `is not distinct from` and a null. PostgreSQL expects that form to match nothing, so it planned every join for one comment and then read the whole posts table once for each of the 20,000 it found: 100 s for one signed-out read of a market's card on a local database (20,000 top-level comments, 102,301 posts), and 1.5 s with the condition written out, most of it the read copying its 100 comments. The condition is now `is null` for a card and `=` for a comment's replies, which also finds the replies through the `contract_replies` index instead of reading the market. The earlier timing run used a market too small next to the posts table to show it. Test: one case in `social-discussion.test.ts`, which runs the scan on 2,000 top-level comments and checks how many PostgreSQL expected and how many rows it read and threw away.
- Reads stop copying, 21 November (not yet on DEV; a follow-up stacked on this branch). A read no longer looks for comments to copy, so the scan of the fifth round is gone, and its test with it.
  - A card's list is one pass over its market's top-level comments in time order. Each entry is the comment's post when it has one, wherever that post sits, and the comment itself when it has none. A comment's list merges the posts under it with the market's replies that have no post, in one statement. The card's list names the id of the comment replied to as its first sort key, which is empty for every top-level comment and changes nothing, so that only the index on (comment replied to, market, time) gives its order. Ordered by time alone, PostgreSQL chose on a test table to walk every market's comments in time order looking for this market's.
  - The lookup by ids (`get-social-posts` with `ids`) returns posts only, as it did. A comment without a post is read through its thread or its own page.
  - The page cursor is the last entry's time and id, with or without a post. It no longer depends on where a scan stopped, and a comment keeps its place when it gets its post, so a reader who is paging sees it once.
  - A card lists its market's comments in the order they were written on the market. For a post written on Yap that is the time of its market comment, which is set a moment after the post's own in the same transaction; the list at the head of the branch used the post's own time. The two differ by milliseconds, and a comment's copy has one time for both.
  - A comment gets its post in the transaction of the first action on it (`materializeSocialPost`), so a refused like, reply, quote, re-Yap, follow, edit, removal or report leaves none. One case is different: a reply that also quotes such a comment, which the API takes and the app does not send. The quoted comment's post is then made in a transaction of its own first, and stays if the reply is refused. In one transaction the second comment's lock would be asked for while a thread's is held, and two requests could wait on each other. A reply that quotes the very comment it replies to has one comment to lock and is not that case.
  - A post is made only in the place the comment is shown: under the comment it replies to, or its trade's or market's card. Only sharing a comment makes it a post of its own. The card is chosen before its thread's lock is held, so it is chosen again with the lock. If the comment's trade got a card of its own in that moment, or the card was removed, the comment is not copied under the card it no longer sits under: the action is refused with "This discussion changed while you were acting on it. Please try again." (or as not found, when the comment has no place left), and nothing is left behind. This holds for a share from the market too. A comment written on its market at such a moment is refused nothing. If its place moved, joining is tried once more, so that the comment joins the thread it now sits in and the people following that thread hear of it. If its place is gone, or moves a second time, it joins no discussion for now: it stays on its market, where Yap lists it, and gets its post on the first action.
  - A comment can get its post between two of a reader's looks. The reader still finds it, the second of two people acting on it at the same moment finds it, and its reply count is right whichever of its replies have posts by then. A read is several statements, so the lists and the counts ask in their own statement what can have changed since the read began: whether the thread is still open, whether the comment the replies sit under can still be seen on its market, and whether the card still names its market. A post taken off Yap, or a comment deleted on its market, after the read began takes no reply written after that, and a card removed after the read began lists nothing. What a read does carry from its first statement is which post or comment it is reading and where that sits (the post above it, its thread and their authors): if a trade gets a card of its own in between, a comment on that trade is read for one more page as sitting under the market's card. Before its list statement a comment's page looks up only what cannot change (which market the comment is on, and that the post is the comment's own copy), so a comment that is hidden and shown again a moment later keeps the replies without posts on its page.
  - Following a thread from a comment without a post gives the comment its post first, as every other action on it does. Giving a comment its post takes the lock of the comment and of every comment above it that has no post, then one thread's lock, and settles where the comment sits. Nothing gives a comment a post, hides it or deletes it without the comment's lock, so a follow is refused if the comment was taken off Yap as it began. Followed without a post, from a look made without those locks, the comment could be placed in a thread it was in at no one moment. Since a follow from a comment can write a post, it has an hourly allowance (300), as a like, a reply, a re-Yap, a quote and a report have. A follow from any other post has none, as before. And the account has to be in use, as for a like or a reply: a deleted account cannot follow from a comment.
  - The check that a person may act in a thread reads that person's blocks again with the thread locked, and adds them to the ones read when the request began. A comment can be given its place by the very request that acts on it, under a card made a moment before. A block with that card's author made before the card existed counts. A reply's quote and the people it mentions, the people an edit mentions, and the creator of a market that a Yap also comments on, are checked against the same list, read after the locks.
  - Older comments could reply to a reply (the market's API stopped taking those in May 2026). They are listed, opened and given posts at any depth. Comments that reply to each other in a circle have no way up to a card and open nothing.
  - Taking back a like made on the market, from a comment that has no post, makes no post. It asks for the comment's lock before the like allowance's, as a like does: asked for in two orders, one person's two requests could wait on each other. For a comment that is not on Yap it answers not found and stops there, so that a card made for the comment's market a moment later cannot turn it into an action that gives the comment a post. If there was no such comment when its lock was asked for, only a post that is there is looked for: a comment written in that moment was not locked first.
  - The reply count's third part and the lists use one condition (`pendingCommentSql`), so the number on a post is the number of entries the reader is shown. Two cases that were counted and never shown are no longer counted: a comment whose thread starts with someone the reader has a block with, and a comment on a trade whose card's author has a block with the commenter. Under a comment's post, the market's replies are listed and counted only if the post is that comment's own copy (written by the comment's author, naming no other market as its source) and the comment can still be seen on its market, because the copier puts replies under no other. The bulk spam tool and a super-ban delete a comment on its market and leave its post on Yap, so this is a state that occurs. A card that a moderator removed, or whose market is no longer public, lists nothing and now counts nothing; it used to keep the number of the posts under it. A comment on a trade whose card is closed while it still names its market is not listed or counted anywhere, and the market's card does not take it instead; no request leaves a trade's card in that state today, since removing one takes its market off it. The count looks a card's market and a thread's first post up by key for the post asked about, and is not sent at all for a page on which no comment has a post. One exception is known: a comment whose author has no account row is counted and not listed, because the count does not look authors up.
  - Through the real handlers on a local database, signed out: opening the card of a market with 250 comments, none on Yap, took 2.0 to 2.4 s and wrote 250 posts and 250 marks on comments. It takes 15 to 49 ms and writes nothing. A page of a market with 20,000 top-level comments took 1.0 to 1.1 s while its comments were still being copied and 0.17 to 0.18 s once all were. It takes 16 ms with none on Yap and 0.12 to 0.13 s with all. Most of that last figure is the reply count for the posts on the page: PostgreSQL compiles that statement before running it on a database with just-in-time compilation on (about 40 ms there, for a statement that runs in about 3 ms without it), at the head of the branch as here.
  - A trade's card costs more than a market's. The comments table has no index on a comment's trade, so the list goes through the market's top-level comments to find the trade's: 4 ms for a trade with three comments and 16 ms for one with 2,857, on a market with 20,000 top-level comments. An index on market, trade and time would make it independent of the market's size; it needs a migration and is not part of this change.
  - A page reads every comment between two entries it shows. A run of comments the reader is not shown (deleted spam, or comments by someone the reader has a block with) is passed over in the same statement: 48 ms for a run of 20,000, about 2.4 ms for each 1,000. It is bounded by the market's size, and it is less than the card's own reply count costs on the same market.
  - Still as it was: a card's reply count goes through its market's comments (0.14 to 0.2 s at 20,000), and a card in the timeline shows reply previews only from comments that have posts. A comment whose content cannot be read as a document is left out of the page it would be on and is still counted. Two comments on different markets with the same id (the migration found none) would each be listed, and neither could be opened.
